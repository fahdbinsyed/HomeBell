HomeBell · discussion for the team

What happens when a school has 5,000 students?

HomeBell works well for a typical school. For a very large one, the pickup screens and the dismissal setup won't hold up as they are. Before we connect the apps to the real server, we need to decide how big schools should run pickup. Your input on how schools actually do this is what decides the design.

The problem

One gate TV can't show a whole school

Today every school has one pickup queue. The gate TV lists every child whose pickup is requested in fixed grade rows. In testing, a 1080p TV showed 30 children across five grades with room to spare; on a 720p screen, or with class and section shown (10 rows), the board now pages through the grades automatically (option A, built). Here is a rough picture of a big school's afternoon. These are estimates, not measured figures.

5,000students
~1,500car pickups a day (many children take the bus or walk)
~30 minwhen most of them arrive
100+children "coming out" at the same moment

One screen can't show 100 names, and parents can't find their child's name in a list that long. The staff queue screen has the same problem: one dispatcher can't manage 1,500 rows.

Where we stand

What already holds up, and what doesn't

Holds up

  • Storing 5,000 students and their guardians. This is small for the database.
  • Live updates to many screens at once. Each screen gets only the changes.
  • Plan limits. A big school can go on a bigger plan.

Doesn't hold up

  • Every TV shows the whole school. A TV can have a gate label, but it doesn't filter anything yet.
  • Dismissal time is one window for the whole school, set per weekday. You can't release KG earlier than Grade 10.
  • The staff queue loads the whole day in one list.
  • Nobody has tested 1,000 parents tapping "Request pickup" within a few minutes.

The options

Four ways to solve it

A · Page through the names on the TV

Built

Keep one queue. When there are more names than fit, the TV flips pages every 6–8 seconds ("Page 1 of 5").

Quick to build, no change for schools

A parent may wait 40 seconds to see their child's name

Doesn't help the staff at all

B · Pickup gates (zones)

Bigger change

The school sets up its gates, like "North gate" and "Main gate", and assigns grades or classes to each. Each TV and each staff member sees only their gate. The parent app tells parents which gate to go to.

Each screen shows 50–100 names, not 1,500

Matches how big schools already split traffic

Parents with children in different gates need a clear answer

More setup for the school

C · Staggered dismissal times

Medium change

Let a dismissal window apply to some grades only: KG at 2:30, primary at 2:45, secondary at 3:00. Parents can't request before their child's window opens.

Spreads the rush over a longer time

Doesn't shrink the screen problem on its own

D · B + C, with A as a safety net

Recommended

Gates split the crowd by place, staggered times split it by time, and paging covers the busy moments on any one screen. This changes the API, so it belongs at the start of Phase 3, before the apps are connected to the real server. Doing it later means building that connection twice.

Your input

Questions we need answered

These decide how option D should work. Real examples from schools you know help most.

  1. How many pickup gates or areas does a big school use, and do staff stand at each one?Decides whether each gate needs its own TV and its own dispatcher.
  2. Are gates assigned by grade, by class, by surname, or does the parent choose?Decides what the school sets up and what the parent sees.
  3. A family has a child in KG and one in Grade 8, in different gates. What happens today?The hardest case. Does the older child walk to the younger one's gate?
  4. Do big schools already release grades at different times? How many minutes apart?Tells us whether staggered windows are needed now or later.
  5. The TV now shows requested children grouped by grade. Is that the right split, or would class and section suit big schools better?A big school may have 8 sections per grade, which makes one grade box very long.
  6. Do children wait in a hall and get called, or wait at the gate?If they wait inside, the hall needs its own screen or a staff tablet.
  7. Is there a real large school we could talk to, or design for first?One real school's routine beats any guess.
